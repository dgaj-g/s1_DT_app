10. Current pedagogical rules that emerged from feedback
--------------------------------------------------------

These rules were not casual preferences. They came from repeated teacher review and should be treated as requirements unless the user explicitly changes them.

Content rules:

- stay inside the Network Technologies topic
- do not leak cyberspace/network security/data transfer content
- every answer must be accurate and teachable
- every question must make sense when read cold by a student
- avoid wording that sounds artificial, vague, or circular

Scoring rules:

- student-marked answers must be objective and predictable
- do not rely on AI marking
- avoid long free-text answers for scored items
- after answer submission, the selected choice should not feel ambiguous or changeable in a way that risks score confusion

Difficulty rules:

- Easy should feel genuinely accessible
- Medium should be a clear step up
- Expert should be demanding but fair, not obscure or self-defeating

Diagram rules:

- diagrams should support the question, not answer it for the student
- labels or embedded text must not reveal the intended answer
- question wording around diagram references should sound natural

Feedback rules:

- "Not quite right" explanations should explain what was wrong clearly
- generic explanation lines were criticised if they did not help the student understand the actual mistake
